---
title: Sessions
description: When to send session_start and session_end, and how metrics are calculated from them.
---

A session is a continuous stretch of time while the app is in the foreground. The app sends two
events with a shared `session_id`:

- `session_start` — when it comes to the foreground;
- `session_end` — when it goes to the background, with a `duration_ms` field.

## Client rules [#client-rules]

1. **A new `session_id` every time the app comes to the foreground.** The UUID is generated on the
   client; both events of a session carry the same `session_id` and `install_id`.
2. **The client calculates `duration_ms`**: time in the foreground in milliseconds, from 0 to
   86,400,000. Use a monotonic clock (`systemUptime`, `SystemClock.elapsedRealtime`,
   `performance.now`), not a wall-clock difference: the user may change the clock during a session.
3. **Short trips to the background end a session too.** The server doesn't stitch sessions across
   pauses. If you want to ignore momentary switches (the notification shade, a system dialog), react
   to "background" rather than "inactive", as the recipes do.
4. **No foreground means activity-based sessions.** For bots and backends, a session is a series of
   user requests with no pauses longer than 30 minutes; see
   [Bots and backends](/docs/recipes/bots-and-backends#server-sessions).

Which lifecycle events start and end a session on each platform is covered in
[Where to initialize analytics](/docs/concepts/initialization).

## How metrics are calculated [#metrics]

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Active | People who had at least one session during the period |
| New | People whose first event in the app fell within the period |
| Sessions | Unique `session_id` values among `session_start` events |
| Avg. duration | Average over sessions with a `session_end`; a session's duration is the maximum `duration_ms` of its `session_end` events |
| Retention D1, D7, … | Share of a cohort of new users who came back exactly 1, 7, … days after their first event |
| Active now | People with a `session_start` in the last 30 minutes whose session hasn't ended yet |

What follows from this:

- **A lost `session_end`** doesn't lose the session: it is counted by `session_start` but left out of
  the average duration. If `session_end` events get lost often, the average duration is understated.
- **Resending** the same event doesn't inflate the numbers: every metric is calculated over unique
  identifiers.
- **Two `session_end` events** for one session (the client recalculated the duration after coming
  back): the maximum is used.
- **A session that spans midnight** counts the person as active on both days, while the session
  itself belongs to the day of its `session_start`.

Days in reports follow the app's time zone, not the device's.

## Version and environment [#context]

The batch `context` describes the device and the build: `platform`, `os_name`, `os_version`,
`app_version`, `app_build`, `device_model`, `device_type`, `locale`, `environment`. An event's
`context` field overrides the batch context field by field. This comes in handy when the offline
queue holds events from an older app version: each event goes out with its own version.

`environment` is `production` (default) or `development`. Reports show production, and development
is turned on with the **Environment** switch. Send `development` from debug builds so that tests don't
skew the numbers.
